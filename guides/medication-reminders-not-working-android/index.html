Why medication reminders fail on Android, and how to fix it

It is rarely the app. Most of the time the reminder does not arrive because the phone closed the app to save battery. Android does this on its own, and many manufacturers add their own battery saving on top.

What happens inside

A medication app schedules exact alarms for each dose. Android allows it, but battery optimization can delay or cancel what an app does in the background. Some manufacturer layers go further: they stop the app from starting by itself after a restart, so nothing places the alarms again.

What we saw in our tests

We test Sonadose on a Huawei Nova 5T and a Samsung Galaxy S23. On the Huawei, if the app is not set to manual in App launch, it stops reminding after a restart. With the setting in place, the alarms come back. Updating the app, on the other hand, does not remove scheduled alarms: we checked by installing a new version over the old one.

The setting for each brand

  • Samsung: in Background usage limits, add the app to “Never auto sleeping apps” and make sure it is not in “Sleeping apps” or “Deep sleeping apps”.
  • Huawei: in App launch, the last item under Battery, turn off “Manage automatically” for the app and keep all three switches on (Auto-launch, Secondary launch, Run in background).
  • Xiaomi: turn on Autostart for the app and set its battery usage to “No restrictions”. This comes from the manufacturer's documentation; we have not tested it on a Xiaomi of our own.

On any brand, also check that the app may show notifications and schedule exact alarms.

How to check it works

The most reliable test is the real one: set a reminder a couple of minutes ahead, lock the phone and wait. Sonadose has this built in under Settings, Reliability check: start the test, lock the phone, and in about a minute a test reminder arrives for you to confirm with Taken. The app's battery guide has the steps for your brand.

This guide is not medical advice. Doses and times are the ones your doctor or pharmacist gave you.
