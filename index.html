Remembering three pills a day is harder than it sounds

It's almost never stubbornness. It's a phone call in the middle of breakfast, a nap that runs long, a new box that looks exactly like the old one.

A paper chart on the fridge doesn't ring. A phone's built-in reminder is one quiet notification, already gone by the time anyone looks at the screen.

Sonadose is built for that moment instead: loud, full screen, and it does not give up after one notification.

How it works

Three steps. Only the first one is your job, and only once.

  1. You set it up

    Add each medicine with a photo of the box, the times it's due, and how many pills come in a pack. A few minutes, once.

  2. They just tap

    At the right time the phone rings and fills the screen: the medicine, the time, and two buttons: Take and Later. Nothing else to find.

  3. You can see it later

    Every confirmed dose lands in a calendar on that phone. One look tells you how the week actually went, with no interrogation over dinner.

Senior mode: two buttons, and nothing to break

Turn it on and the app becomes a single screen. No tabs, no menus, no settings to wander into by accident.

  • Text at a size you can read across the kitchen. The whole screen scales up, not just the heading.
  • A photo of the actual box. The right pack is obvious even when every name looks alike.
  • An alarm that really rings. Full screen, like an alarm clock, even when the phone is on silent.
  • A guarded exit. A stray tap can't drop them into settings and lose the app.

Big buttons for them. Peace of mind for you.

Sonadose runs on their phone: big buttons, one screen, no account and no signal needed. The reminders don't depend on us — if our server is down, their phone still rings.

When you want to know it's going well, they share the profile with you. Sharing starts with a code generated on their phone and handed over once — nobody can request access. From then on you see the medicines and whether each dose was taken, and you can be told when one is missed, or when their phone has been quiet for a long time.

What you don't get is their phone. Sharing shows the history; it does not let you change a schedule, and whoever owns the profile can withdraw access at any time. The app is free.

Built to still be there at nine in the evening

A reminder that quietly stops working is worse than no reminder at all. That's the part of Sonadose that gets the most attention.

  • Survives reboots and battery savers

    Exact-time alarms are re-armed after a restart, and the setup asks for exactly the permissions Android needs to let them fire on time.

  • Keeps asking

    The alarm repeats up to five times, five minutes apart. After that the reminder stays on screen until the intake window closes. Snooze for fifteen minutes when now isn't the moment.

  • A reliability check built in

    Send yourself a test reminder, and follow a step-by-step guide to the battery settings for your phone's brand. Huawei and Xiaomi need a hand.

  • Counts what's left

    Tell it the pack size and Sonadose counts down, then reminds you to buy the next box before the last pill goes.

What leaves the phone: almost nothing

Your medication list, schedules and intake history are kept on the device, and no account is needed to use the app. Medication and profile names are never sent to analytics or advertisers, and your data is never sold.

Two things do leave the phone, and neither is medical: crash and usage statistics through Google Firebase, and a licence check with RevenueCat that tells the app whether Premium is active. Both carry a technical identifier for the installation, not for you. The detail is in the privacy policy.

Questions people actually ask

Is Sonadose free?

Yes, and there is nothing to buy. There is no paid tier, no subscription and no advertising. Every profile, every medication, the full reminder engine, and family sharing with its alerts are all included. Nothing about the reminding is held back.

How is this different from a pill box?

A pill box organises the week, but it cannot remind anyone. Dispensers with a built-in alarm can, except they have to be bought and then carried around.

Sonadose does the same job on the phone they already own: a full-screen alarm that repeats, and a history you can look back at.

Which phones does it work on?

Android 8.0 (Oreo) and newer. There's no iPhone version. Reminder delivery is tested hardest on Samsung and Huawei, the brands most aggressive about shutting down background apps.

Does it need an internet connection?

No. The schedule, the reminders and the history all work offline, and no account is needed for any of it. A connection is used to install the app and — only if you switch it on — to share a profile with someone.

Will it ring if the phone is on silent?

Yes. The reminder takes over the whole screen and rings on the alarm channel, like an alarm clock, so silent mode doesn't mute it.

Android asks for a couple of permissions the first time (exact alarms and full-screen notifications), and the setup walks through them one at a time.

What happens if they tap “Later”?

Sonadose comes back in fifteen minutes and repeats twice more. Nothing is ever silently marked as taken, and Later stays available until the intake window closes.

Can I set it up on my own phone for them?

Yes. Plenty of people run Sonadose on their own phone as a personal reminder, or set up a parent's phone and hand it back.

Without an account the schedule stays on the phone it was set up on. With one, it follows your own phones, and a parent can share their profile so you see the medicines and whether each dose was taken.

Are there ads?

No. There are no ads in Sonadose today, and there will never be one on a reminder, an alarm or the elder-mode screen.

What does Sonadose not do?

It doesn't give medical advice, suggest doses, or check whether two medicines interact. It's a reminder organizer. The medical questions belong with a doctor or a pharmacist.
